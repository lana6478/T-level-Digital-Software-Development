# Occupational Specialism Overview

*Digital Software Development T Level → Occupational Specialism*

Unlike some T Levels, Digital Software Development has only **one** occupational specialism - also called Digital Software Development - rather than a choice between several. Every student on this T Level studies the same specialism, worth 50% of the overall qualification alongside the shared [Core Component](../01-core-component/00-overview.md). It prepares students for the specialist area of digital software development, building toward roles such as software developer/engineer within a wider development team (see [Analyse a Problem, 1.2](01-analyse-a-problem.md#12-roles-and-responsibilities-in-the-digital-team) for how that role fits alongside product owners, scrum masters, technical leads, UX/UI designers and testers).

## The eight content areas

1. **[Analyse a Problem](01-analyse-a-problem.md)** - be able to analyse a problem to define requirements and acceptance criteria aligned to user needs.
2. **[Ethics and Risk](02-ethics-and-risk.md)** - apply ethical principles and manage risks in line with legal and regulatory requirements when developing software.
3. **[Sources of Knowledge](03-sources-of-knowledge.md)** - discover, evaluate and apply reliable sources of knowledge.
4. **[Design](04-design.md)** - understand and apply common design approaches, platforms and principles to design a software solution.
5. **Create solutions in a social and collaborative environment** - working effectively as part of a development team.
6. **Implement a solution using at least two appropriate languages** - building working software.
7. **Testing a software solution** - planning and carrying out testing.
8. **Change, maintain and support software** - looking after software once it's live.

Content areas 1-4 are covered on this site's pages linked above; content areas 5-8 and the Scheme of Assessment are covered on their own pages, linked below.
