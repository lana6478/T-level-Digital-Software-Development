# Ethics and Risk

*Digital Software Development T Level → Occupational Specialism → Ethics and Risk*

This is Content Area 2 of the Digital Software Development occupational specialism: applying ethical principles and managing risk in line with legal and regulatory requirements when developing software.

## 2.1 Legal and regulatory requirements

Students investigate and apply the legal and regulatory considerations relevant to the context they're developing software in, including: intellectual property rights and licences, consumer protection, age ratings and classifications, advertising laws, data protection and privacy, copyright and patent law, gambling legislation, staff/employment responsibilities, territorial restrictions, system security, and equality and diversity. They also investigate and apply relevant development standards, such as **ISO/IEC/IEEE 90003:2018** (software engineering guidance) and **W3C** (web standards).

Alongside the legal side, students consider the ethical implications of software development: code of conduct, professional practice, software licensing, and inclusion and diversity. *(Signposted competencies: E5, M4-M6, D1, D5, D6.)*

## 2.2 Identifying and managing risk

Students assess the risks of developing a software product in its specific context and market:

- **Risk identification** covers: data/system security (malicious vs. accidental damage), compatibility with other systems, speed of development, meeting functional and non-functional requirements, meeting KPIs, legal and ethical considerations, user engagement, and product reach.
- **Risk assessment** weighs likelihood against seriousness, and considers the risk's potential impact.
- Students then identify ways to **mitigate** each risk, build **contingency plans**, and set up **ongoing monitoring**.
- They also investigate the policies and procedures organisations use to manage and mitigate risk: backup, security, confidentiality/integrity/availability (**CIA**), personnel/skills/training, business continuity planning, and disaster recovery planning.

Finally, students need to be able to make and justify software development decisions and recommendations, based on properly weighing risk against reward for the specific context and market they're working in. *(E5, M4-M6, D1, D5, D6.)*

## Key terms

- **CIA** - confidentiality, integrity and availability, the three core properties an information security policy protects.
- **ISO/IEC/IEEE 90003:2018** - a standard giving guidance on applying software engineering practice.
- **Business continuity planning** - planning to keep critical operations running during and after a disruptive incident.
- **Disaster recovery planning** - planning to recover and restore systems/data after a disruptive incident.
- **Contingency plan** - a predefined backup plan for when a risk materialises.
