# What Are T Levels, and What Is Digital Software Development?

*Digital Software Development T Level → Overview*

T Levels are two-year, Level 3 technical study programmes for 16-19 year-olds, sitting alongside A Levels and Apprenticeships as one of the main post-16 routes in England. This page explains what a T Level is in general, and what the **T Level Technical Qualification in Digital Software Development (Level 3)** covers specifically.

## T Levels as a qualification type

T Levels combine:

- **Classroom-based theory and practical learning**, delivered through a Technical Qualification (TQ).
- **A minimum of 315 hours of industry placement** with an employer, giving students real workplace experience.

T Level programmes are developed with employers so the content matches what industry actually needs, preparing students for skilled work, an Apprenticeship, or higher-level study, including university.

## The Technical Qualification (TQ)

The **Technical Qualification** is the classroom-based, examined half of the T Level, awarded by Pearson. It is built from **Outline Content** produced by T Level employer, professional body and Provider panels, based on the relevant Apprenticeship Standards. Pearson elaborates that Outline Content into a full specification that:

- gives Providers (schools/colleges) an accurate, teachable interpretation of what must be covered and assessed;
- is designed to let students reach **threshold competence** in the Occupational Specialism;
- integrates English, maths and digital competencies directly into the taught content (see [Competency Frameworks](../03-appendices/01-competency-frameworks.md)) - these are signposted against the Occupational Specialism content using codes like E1, M1 and D1, because that's where students get the most natural opportunity to develop them.

## What "Digital Software Development" covers

This T Level replaces the earlier *T Level Technical Qualification in Digital Production, Design and Development*. It is aimed at students who want a career building software: coding, problem-solving, and creating digital solutions. Unlike some T Levels, it has only **one** Occupational Specialism (rather than a choice of several) - Digital Software Development itself. Students study a shared **Core Component** covering problem solving, introduction to programming, legislation and regulatory requirements, business context, data, digital environments, security and emerging issues, then go deeper into the Digital Software Development specialism.

## Qualification purpose

The purpose of the qualification is to give students the knowledge and skills to progress into highly skilled employment, an Apprenticeship, or higher-level study (including university) in digital software development. By the end, students should have reached **threshold competence**: they hold the core knowledge and skills for the sector and specialism, and are ready to build toward full occupational competence with further support once they're in a job.

## Key terms

- **T Level** - a two-year Level 3 technical study programme combining a Technical Qualification with an industry placement.
- **Technical Qualification (TQ)** - the classroom-taught, examined component of a T Level.
- **Outline Content** - the employer-panel-defined content framework (based on Apprenticeship Standards) that Pearson turns into the full specification.
- **Threshold competence** - having the core knowledge and skills needed to be ready to develop full occupational competence on the job, rather than being fully competent from day one.
- **Occupational Specialism** - the specific occupational pathway a student specialises in during their TQ. This T Level has only one: Digital Software Development.
- **Industry placement** - the minimum 315-hour employer placement that runs alongside the Technical Qualification.
